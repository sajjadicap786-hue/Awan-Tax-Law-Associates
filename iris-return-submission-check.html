IRIS FILING · PRACTICAL SOLUTIONS

Is your Pakistan tax return
actually submitted?

A practical checklist for distinguishing saved work from completed filing.

7 October 2026 · By Awan Tax Law Associates

Start by checking the status

Finishing the figures and saving a form can feel like the end of filing. Before you put your records away, take a moment to check what happened to the return. This is especially useful if you filed for the first time, asked someone else to prepare it, or stopped midway through an online session.

FBR’s published filing guidance says successful submission of the income tax return and wealth statement is confirmed when both forms move from Draft to Completed Task. Use that status check, rather than assuming that a saved screen or a payment alone proves submission. The forms relevant to your taxpayer category and circumstances should be reviewed separately.

A five-step review

  1. Open your own IRIS account. Use the official portal and keep your login credentials private. If a consultant assisted you, ask for the filing documents and review them together.
  2. Check the taxpayer and tax period. Match the name, identification details and year to the work you intended to complete. A completed form for an older period does not answer the question for the year you are checking.
  3. Find the relevant completed forms. Review Completed Task and compare it with Draft. Where a wealth statement applies, check it as well as the return. If something remains in Draft, establish what still needs attention.
  4. Keep readable copies. Download the available submitted documents and retain them with your income certificates, bank records and preparation notes. Use a folder name that includes the tax year so next year’s review is easier.
  5. Review the figures, not just the status. Submission status helps establish completion; it does not prove that every entry is correct. Compare the main figures with your supporting records and ask about anything unfamiliar.

If your form is still in Draft

Write down what you see before changing anything: the period, form name and any validation message. Check whether the preparation was interrupted or a necessary detail remains unresolved. Avoid creating several new drafts simply to experiment. A screenshot of the message can help a reviewer understand the issue, but hide identification numbers and account details before sharing it publicly.

For someone filing from overseas, also note your time zone and the best time for a call. Online review can start with the status and documents you already have; there is no need to wait until a visit to Pakistan to ask what the screen means.

Keep payments and filing evidence together

Organise payment records alongside the return documents instead of treating them as interchangeable evidence. A useful folder has separate sections for income, deductions, assets and liabilities, payments, and submitted forms. This makes it easier to explain which document supports which figure.

If your records show an unexpected entry, prepare a specific question: Which document supports this amount? Which period does it belong to? Has the same receipt been counted twice? Specific questions are more useful than asking whether the whole return “looks fine.”

Questions people often ask

Does a screenshot of a saved form prove submission?

Use the Completed Task check described by FBR and retain the available submitted forms. A screenshot without the year and status may leave important questions unanswered.

Can someone help me review the result remotely?

Yes. Awan Tax Law Associates works online with clients in Pakistan and worldwide on Pakistan income tax returns. Start by telling us the tax year, what has already been prepared and the problem you want reviewed.

What should I send in my first enquiry?

A short description is enough to start: your income type, the year, whether the form appears in Draft or Completed Task, and the wording of any error. Agree on a private document-sharing channel before sending sensitive records.
