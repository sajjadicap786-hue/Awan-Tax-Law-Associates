Your first Pakistan tax return: a practical starting guide

A clear preparation plan for people who want to file but do not know where to begin.

PREPARATION GUIDE · 6 OCTOBER 2026

Start with what you know, not with a form

Many first-time filers begin by opening a filing screen and trying to decide which number belongs in each box. A more useful starting point is a short account of your financial situation. Write down your employment, business activity, freelance work, rental income and any other receipts. Identify the year you want reviewed and note whether you already have a taxpayer registration. This summary is the starting point for an informed conversation.

Make a record of every income source

If you changed jobs, had two employers, received rent or worked independently alongside employment, mention each source. Keep supporting certificates and payment records together. Do not assume that a single salary certificate answers questions about all your receipts. Equally, a movement between your own accounts should be identified as a transfer so it can be understood in context. The task is to explain the records accurately, rather than describe every bank entry as income.

Build a simple folder for your documents

Create a folder for the relevant tax year. Inside it, keep income records, bank statements, deduction certificates and documents for major transactions. Add earlier returns or statements if any exist. Give files clear names so a document can be matched to the period and source it describes. A list of missing items is useful too: it tells the preparer which figures need clarification before work can proceed.

Discuss registration before creating another profile

Some people already have registration information from a previous enquiry or business activity. Tell us what is available before treating the task as a first-time registration. If you cannot access an existing account, explain the issue without sending passwords or one-time codes in your first message. Registration support and return preparation can be discussed together, but the work required should be clear.

Expect questions about your circumstances

Questions are part of careful preparation. A change in employment, property purchase, loan repayment or a major transfer may need an explanation and supporting record. If you are unsure of a figure, say so. Guessing creates a different problem from openly identifying missing information. We review what is available and discuss how any gap affects the next step.

Review the prepared details before submission

Check your personal particulars, income figures and the information behind them. Ask for an explanation of entries you do not understand. You should have the opportunity to correct inaccurate information and confirm the prepared details before approving submission assistance. Keep the acknowledgement and the supporting documents afterwards; filing is easier to follow when the records remain organised.

Agree on the service scope and fee

The amount of work depends on your circumstances and the quality of the records. A simple salary case and a return with several income sources may require different preparation. Ask which tasks are included, what documents are needed and how timing will be discussed. A clear agreement at the beginning helps avoid uncertainty about what happens next.

A useful first enquiry

You can write: “I want help with my first Pakistan tax return for [year]. My income comes from [sources], and I have [registration details / no registration details] available.” Mention any specific concern, such as missing certificates or a major transaction. This provides context without including confidential documents in your first message.

This guide helps you prepare for a discussion. It does not decide your tax liability or filing obligation. Review current requirements and your own circumstances before acting.
