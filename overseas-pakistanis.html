PAKISTAN TAX MATTERS, MANAGED FROM ABROAD

Living overseas.
Staying organised in Pakistan.

Living outside Pakistan does not always mean your Pakistan tax affairs can be left unattended. You may own a rented property, hold investments, operate a business, receive payments in a Pakistani account or need to review an earlier filing. Awan Tax Law Associates assists overseas Pakistanis with Pakistan income tax return preparation and related compliance matters through online communication and an agreed document-sharing process.

Our service is designed for people who need a clear explanation of their Pakistan position while living in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain, Kuwait, the United Kingdom, Europe, the United States, Canada, Australia or another country. These are locations from which you can contact us online, not a claim that we maintain offices abroad. Our office is in Rawalpindi, Pakistan, and our work on this page concerns Pakistan tax matters.

Start by understanding your filing position

An overseas address is a starting fact, not a complete tax assessment. Tell us where you live, the tax year involved, your visits to Pakistan and the income or transactions you want reviewed. We also ask whether you have filed a Pakistan return before and whether an existing taxpayer profile is available. The initial discussion helps identify which matters require a review and what records are needed before preparation.

For some clients, the enquiry concerns a current return. For others, it begins with a registration problem, a tax deduction record, a property transaction or a notice. We do not assume that all overseas Pakistanis have identical filing requirements. The proposed scope and service fee are discussed after understanding your circumstances.

Residence status needs a year-specific review

Tax residence and the place you currently live are related but are not interchangeable. We review your travel history and relevant circumstances against the rules applicable to the tax year. Keep a record of arrival and departure dates, particularly when you make several visits or have returned to Pakistan for an extended period. A residence review should precede conclusions about income treatment.

A NICOP, foreign visa or overseas job can form part of the background information, but we do not use one document alone to promise a particular tax outcome. If your circumstances changed during the year, explain the change and provide the documents available. See the FBR overview of income tax concepts; current legislation should be checked for the year being reviewed.

Pakistan-source income while you live abroad

An overseas Pakistani may still have Pakistan-source income, such as rent from property in Pakistan or returns from certain Pakistan investments. We help identify the income streams that need review, organise their supporting records and distinguish them from other receipts. The source and nature of the income matter, as well as your residence status and the relevant year.

List every income source rather than sending only a single account total. Explain rent, business receipts, investment payments and any other material entries separately. If an account receives both your own transfers and income payments, label the transfers so they are not mistaken for additional earnings. This creates a clearer foundation for preparation.

Rental property and property transaction records

If you receive rent from Pakistan, gather the tenancy agreement, rent schedule, payment evidence and available withholding certificates. Identify each property and explain shared ownership, changes in tenants, annual increments and vacant periods. A family member managing the property may help assemble records, but the information should still be reviewed with you before it is used.

For a purchase or sale, retain the transaction documents, dates, consideration and available tax payment evidence. Tell us whether your question concerns return preparation, deduction records or advance tax at transfer. FBR publishes a separate facilitation process for qualifying overseas Pakistanis in relation to property-transfer advance tax. Eligibility and approval should be reviewed before relying on that process; it is not a general assurance about every filing obligation. Refer to FBR’s overseas property-transaction FAQs.

Overseas earnings and money sent to Pakistan

Income earned abroad, a transfer between your own accounts and money received by a family member describe different events. Tell us where funds originated, who sent and received them and the purpose of the transfer. Do not assume that every overseas bank credit has the same treatment, or that describing a payment as a remittance resolves every tax question. We review the facts and relevant documentation before discussing your position.

Keep transfer receipts, bank statements and available remittance certificates together. Where funds supported a property purchase, investment or another major transaction, retain the records connecting the transfer with that use. This section is an invitation to organise evidence for review, not a promise that all foreign receipts are exempt or that a certificate guarantees a particular outcome.

Foreign salary and foreign tax documentation

If your enquiry involves overseas employment income, describe where the work was performed and provide the relevant income documentation for review. Keep foreign tax records where available and mention any Pakistan income received during the same period. Residence status, income source and any relevant treaty or relief provisions need consideration before drawing conclusions.

Our Pakistan service does not automatically include filing a return in your country of residence. If you need advice or filing assistance under another country’s laws, that is a separate matter to discuss with an appropriately qualified adviser there. We can organise the Pakistan documents within the agreed scope, but do not promise relief, credit or exemption without reviewing the applicable conditions.

Wealth statements and disclosure questions

The statements and disclosures relevant to a return should be assessed for your particular circumstances. We do not automatically apply the same checklist to resident and non-resident clients. Tell us about assets, liabilities, major transactions and previous statements so we can identify the information that needs review for the relevant year.

If a statement is required within the agreed engagement, we organise the supporting details and explain the prepared figures for your approval. A property bought using overseas savings, a loan repayment or a substantial investment should be supported by records that explain the transaction. Where documents are missing, we discuss the gap instead of inserting unsupported figures.

NTN registration, profile information and access issues

Some overseas clients already have a Pakistan taxpayer profile but have not used it recently. Others are beginning their filing journey. Tell us what registration information you have and whether the issue concerns first-time registration, profile particulars or access to an existing account. Checking the background helps avoid treating an existing profile as a new registration request.

Keep your contact information accurate and describe any access difficulty in your initial message. Do not send passwords or one-time codes in a public comment or first enquiry. We agree on an appropriate process after understanding the task. Registration support and annual return preparation are related services, but their respective scope should be clear.

ATL status and earlier returns

If you want to understand your Active Taxpayer List position, we can discuss a status check alongside the filing records available. Provide acknowledgements for the years you have filed and explain any gaps. Status review, return preparation and correction of an earlier filing are different tasks; we agree on the relevant work rather than assume one service covers all three.

Tell us if you believe a previous return contains an error or omitted information. We review the records before discussing available next steps. A filing history should be examined year by year, and no blanket assurance is given about an earlier return solely because an acknowledgement exists.

FBR notices received while you are overseas

Being abroad can make it easier to overlook a message or notice. If you receive one, retain the complete document, attachments, stated dates and earlier correspondence. Arrange a private review of the full request rather than sending only a cropped screenshot. Tell us whether a reply has already been submitted and provide its acknowledgement.

We help organise the information requested and discuss the scope of response-preparation assistance after review. A notice may relate to a particular year or transaction, so keep the corresponding records available. We do not promise a result before reading the notice and understanding the evidence.

A practical online process

  1. Initial enquiry: Share your country of residence, the tax year, income types and the service you need.
  2. Scope and documents: We discuss the work, fee and a checklist suited to your situation.
  3. Private sharing: Agree on a document-sharing channel and provide the relevant records.
  4. Preparation and questions: We organise the information and ask about gaps or unclear entries.
  5. Your review: Check the prepared details and ask questions before approving submission assistance.
  6. Keep your records: Retain the available acknowledgement and the documents used in preparation.

Let us know your time zone and preferred contact time when you message. A mutually suitable communication time can be discussed; this is not a promise of round-the-clock availability. Where a family member helps gather Pakistan documents, clarify their role so the review and approval process remains clear.

How fees and timing are discussed

An overseas return involving only one income source may differ from an engagement that includes residence review, property transactions, several accounts or previous filing questions. The fee is discussed according to the proposed work. Preparation timing depends on receiving the relevant documents and resolving questions promptly. We explain the scope before starting and discuss additional work separately if it arises.

Send a useful first message

You can begin with: “I live in [country] and need help with my Pakistan return for [tax year]. My Pakistan income includes [sources], and I have [filed / not filed] previously.” Add any specific question about property, remittances, residence status or a notice. This provides useful context without including confidential documents in your first message.
