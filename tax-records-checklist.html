TAX GUIDES & PRACTICAL READING

Organise your tax records before preparation begins

A practical checklist for salaries, business receipts, rental income, remittances and major transactions.

PREPARATION GUIDE · 6 OCTOBER 2026

Identify the year and keep periods clear

Label records with the year being reviewed and check the period each document covers. Do not mix a current bank statement with an earlier year’s income certificate without explaining the difference. Keeping dates visible helps identify information gaps and prevents the same record being used twice in a working schedule.

Salaried income records

Gather employer income certificates and relevant deduction details. If you changed employment, keep records from each employer. Mention additional work, rent or other income alongside salary. A payslip can help explain a payment, but the complete record for the relevant period is more useful for preparation.

Business and professional records

Organise receipts, invoices, purchases and expenses with their supporting documents. Explain the activity you perform and how payments are recorded. Identify borrowing or transfers separately from customer receipts. If personal and business transactions share an account, a clear description helps the reviewer understand which event each entry represents.

Freelance and overseas payments

Keep client invoices or platform statements with the corresponding bank receipts. Explain fees, combined payments and currency differences. Retain available remittance documentation and describe the origin and purpose of funds. A receipt from abroad is not a complete description of the underlying transaction, so include the work or transfer context.

Rental property records

Prepare a property-by-property record of tenancy periods, agreed rent and payments received. Mention increases, late payments, vacancies and shared ownership. Keep agreements and available withholding evidence together. Identify deposits separately and explain the arrangement rather than adding all property-related receipts into one undifferentiated total.

Assets, borrowing and substantial transactions

Keep documents for property purchases, vehicle sales, investments and loans where relevant to the review. Note dates, amounts and the source or use of funds. If a family member helped finance a transaction, explain it and retain the evidence available. Do not assume a large payment can be understood solely from its bank description.

Prior returns and statements

Earlier filings can provide context, particularly when information carries forward or circumstances have changed. Retain acknowledgements and the prepared statements, not just a screenshot of a submission message. Tell us which earlier figures you are uncertain about so those questions can be considered explicitly.

Make a missing-records list

A short list of unavailable items is better than an unexplained blank. We discuss which gaps affect the preparation and what further evidence may be needed. Use clear file names and avoid editing original documents to match a working calculation. Keep originals available so the evidence remains understandable.

Share records through an agreed channel

Your first enquiry should describe the service and the year. Agree on document sharing before sending confidential records. Do not put CNIC images, account passwords or one-time codes in public comments. After preparation, retain the documents, reviewed figures and available acknowledgement together.

This guide helps you prepare for a discussion. It does not decide your tax liability or filing obligation. Review current requirements and your own circumstances before acting.
